How a web page works

A short tour of the layers behind every screen you read. Mark it up with your Pencil.

Why pages look the way they do

A web page is a conversation between a server, a browser and a person. The server sends text, the browser turns it into boxes, and the person decides in a few seconds whether the boxes are worth reading. Colour carries meaning before words do. A red button says stop or danger, a green one says go, and a faded link says it is not important. Contrast decides whether anyone can read it at all.

Good layout is mostly restraint. A single column of text, generous margins and a clear hierarchy of headings do more for comprehension than any amount of decoration. Forms are where pages ask something back. Labels, error messages and sensible defaults are the difference between a signup and an abandoned tab.
